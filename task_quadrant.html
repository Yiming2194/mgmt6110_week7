My reflection · Draft reply emails to job applicants

(a) Where it sits

Agents run it, a person judges (top right)

(b) Why

A reply email to a job application has no single right answer. The agent independently runs the workflow when a new job application that meets the predefined search conditions comes in, and it creates the draft reply. A standardised reply email may not always be appropriate and relevant, so deciding how to respond to applicants requires human judgement. During the test runs, a job application email that contained no attachment failed to trigger the Zap, highlighting the limitation of predefined search conditions. So this is work that still requires human and AI to work together, so that no applications are overlooked and the responses are appropriate and relevant.

(c) Who judges, and what would move it

Recruiters remain the final gatekeeper, checking for missed applications and reviewing draft replies before sending. The agent can independently detect matching emails and generate the standardised reply email for human follow-up. The workflow can be moved to "AI alone, with checks" at the bottom left if it can go beyond keyword matching and has the ability to understand, interpret and respond appropriately to every job application email, allowing recruiters to do periodic checks instead of reviewing every response.

(d) On my group's placement

I generally agree with my group's placement of the four agents. For example, "Insurance: Check completeness of claims documents" fits "AI alone, with checks" because the task involves verifying documents against predefined requirements, with a clear right answer. Similarly, "Operations: Escalation of disputes" fits "Agents run it, a person judges", as resolving disputes often requires contextual understanding and human judgement. I also agree that fiscal budgeting requires human decision-making, with AI supporting the analysis of financial information.

However, I would reconsider placing financial analyst research under "AI alone, with checks". Financial research typically involves interpreting market trends, assessing risks, and making judgements based on uncertain information, so it can be work with no single right answer. Besides, when the research informs important financial decisions, it becomes work that people and AI must do together. While AI can efficiently gather and analyse data, human analysts are needed to validate findings, interpret business implications, and exercise judgement. Therefore, I would place financial analyst research under "Agents run it, a person judges" instead.
